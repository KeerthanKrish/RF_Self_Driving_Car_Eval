# Real-World Driving Datasets for Imitation-Learning Warm-Starts

`02_rl_algorithms.md` and `06_recommendations.md` both flagged that serious systems almost never train RL from a blank slate — they warm-start from imitation learning on logged real driving data, or run offline RL directly against it. This file surveys the actual datasets that make that possible, with practical notes on size, licensing, and access friction specifically relevant to a solo developer (not a lab with institutional data-use agreements), and how they're typically combined with RL in practice.

## Quick comparison table

| Dataset | Data type | Scale | License | Sign-up friction | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD)** | Vectorized agent trajectories + map, no raw sensor data | 100,000+ segments (20s each, 10Hz), ~570 hours, 1,750 km of roads across 6 US cities | Waymo Dataset License Agreement (non-commercial research) | Register at waymo.com/open, agree to license — no institutional affiliation required | Feeds Waymax, GPUDrive, Nocturne directly (`01`); the standard substrate for offline-RL-on-logged-data work |
| **Waymo Open Dataset (Perception)** | Raw sensor data (camera + LiDAR) + 3D labels | ~2,000 segments, 20s each | Same license family, non-commercial by default (commercial license available on request to Waymo) | Same as above | Perception-model training, less directly relevant to RL/planning than WOMD |
| **nuScenes** | Full sensor suite (6 cameras, 5 radars, 1 LiDAR, 360°) + 3D annotations | 1,000 scenes, 20s each, ~1.44M camera images, ~400GB total | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial) | Free account + agree to terms of use at nuscenes.org | Perception + short-horizon prediction; less commonly used directly for RL than WOMD/nuPlan |

| **comma2k19** | Monocular road-facing camera + GPS/IMU + full CAN bus data | 2,019 one-minute segments (~33 hours total), ~100GB, downloadable in ~10GB chunks | Openly released by comma.ai (research-friendly; check current terms before any commercial use) | Direct download from GitHub/Hugging Face, no account required | The lightest-weight, most solo-developer-accessible dataset on this list; real-world highway driving (CA-280), used historically to develop openpilot |

## The access/friction reality, for a solo developer specifically

The good news: **none of Waymo/nuScenes/nuPlan/Argoverse require institutional affiliation or an approval committee** — this isn't like some medical or restricted government datasets where a university email or signed data-use agreement reviewed by a legal team is a prerequisite. A personal email address, a checkbox agreeing to non-commercial research terms, and (for the Waymo family) a short registration form are the actual barriers. The real friction is **bandwidth and disk space**, not paperwork:
- nuPlan's *full* sensor dataset (200+TB) is simply not realistic for a solo developer to download or store — but the **released 10% sensor subset (~16TB) is still large**, and critically, **the trajectory/map-only logs (no raw sensor data) are a much smaller, much more tractable download** and are what actually matters if your interest is planning/motion-level RL rather than perception-from-raw-sensors.
- WOMD and Argoverse 2's motion-forecasting subsets are vectorized (positions/headings/velocities, not raw camera/LiDAR frames) specifically because that's what's needed for motion prediction and planning research — this makes them dramatically smaller and more solo-developer-tractable than the corresponding perception datasets, and is exactly why Waymax/GPUDrive/Nocturne (all covered in `01`) build on WOMD rather than the raw Perception Dataset.
- **comma2k19 is the standout for pure "can I actually download and poke at this on a laptop today" accessibility** — 100GB total, no account gate, chunked into ~10GB pieces, and it's the dataset underlying comma.ai's own real-world openpilot development, giving it a credible track record outside a research lab. Its scope is narrow (California highway driving only, camera + CAN + IMU, no HD map or other-agent trajectory labels) — genuinely useful for learning end-to-end camera-to-control mappings, much less useful if your interest is urban intersections/traffic/pedestrians, where the vectorized WOMD/nuPlan/Argoverse family is the better fit.

One important nuance on licensing: **"non-commercial research" is the default across essentially this entire dataset family** (Waymo, nuScenes, nuPlan, Argoverse's CC BY-NC-SA data). This is entirely fine for a personal research/learning project exactly like this one, but is a real constraint to remember if this project's scope ever shifts toward something monetized — at that point, re-checking each dataset's current commercial-licensing terms (Waymo explicitly offers a separate commercial license on request; others may not) becomes a real prerequisite, not a footnote.

## `trajdata`: the unifying access layer

Writing four separate parsers for WOMD's TFRecord format, nuScenes' relational-foreign-key structure, Lyft Level 5's zarr files, and Argoverse's format is real, tedious engineering work with no research value of its own. [`NVlabs/trajdata`](https://github.com/NVlabs/trajdata) exists specifically to remove this: a single unified Python interface across nuScenes, WOMD, Lyft Level 5, INTERACTION, and others, installable with dataset-specific extras (`trajdata[nusc]`, `trajdata[waymo]`, etc.), presenting all of them through one consistent trajectory/map data format. For a solo developer, this is the single highest-leverage tool on this list — it converts "I need to learn N different dataset APIs" into "I need to learn one," and is directly what several of the offline-RL-on-real-data papers cited below build on rather than writing bespoke parsers.

Sources: [trajdata paper (2307.13924)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13924), [trajdata GitHub](https://github.com/NVlabs/trajdata).

## How these datasets actually get combined with RL in practice — three patterns

### Pattern 1: behavior cloning pretrain → RL fine-tune
The pattern already covered in `02` via ApolloRL: pretrain a policy with plain supervised behavior cloning on logged expert trajectories, then continue training with RL (online, in a simulator) from that warm start rather than from scratch. This directly mitigates BC's compounding-error problem (small mistakes push the policy into states never seen in the demonstrations) by giving RL a competent starting point to refine rather than requiring it to discover basic competence through pure exploration. ApolloRL's own reported caveat is worth repeating here: even with this warm-start, the resulting agent could not directly replace the production system or an experienced human driver — a realistic expectation-setter for what this pattern actually buys you.

### Pattern 2: offline RL directly on the logged dataset, no live simulation at all
Rather than using logged data merely to *initialize* a policy that's then refined via live environment interaction, offline RL methods (Conservative Q-Learning, per `02`) learn directly and entirely from the static dataset, with **no further environment interaction of any kind**. This matters because it sidesteps the "online exploration in real traffic is unsafe" problem (`05`) at its root, rather than mitigating it. The concrete numbers, worth restating here since they're the clearest quantified case for this pattern: applying CQL with an entity-centric transformer state representation on WOMD achieved roughly a **3× improvement in success rate and 6-7× reduction in collision rate** versus the strongest behavior-cloning baseline, evaluated on 1,000 held-out unseen scenarios. [`AntonioAlgaida/WaymoOfflineRL`](https://github.com/AntonioAlgaida/WaymoOfflineRL) (`07`) reproduces this comparison directly and is a concrete, clonable starting point if this pattern specifically interests you.

### Pattern 3: use the logged data as the simulator itself (log-replay / closed-loop reactive)
Rather than pretraining a policy and later dropping it into a separately-authored simulator, nuPlan and Waymax (per `01`) use the logged dataset *as* the environment: other agents either non-reactively replay their exact logged trajectories, or (in nuPlan-R's case) react via a learned model trained on that same logged distribution. This is architecturally distinct from patterns 1-2 — it's less "warm-start a policy" and more "the dataset defines the world your policy is evaluated (and can be trained) inside," and it's the pattern that makes the Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge's realism-matching evaluation (`05`) meaningful in the first place, since "does simulated traffic behave like real logged traffic" is only a well-posed question when the simulator is genuinely built from that same real data.

## Scoping note: these datasets pair much more naturally with one simulator family than another

Nearly everything in this file (WOMD, nuPlan, Argoverse's motion-forecasting subset) is **vectorized trajectory/kinematic data, not photorealistic pixels**. This means these datasets pair naturally with the Waymax/GPUDrive/nuPlan/trajdata track from `01` — abstracted state, continuous action, real logged topology — and considerably less naturally with CARLA, whose entire value proposition is photorealistic rendering that none of these datasets provide (you'd need CARLA's Digital Twin Tool or hand-authored maps to get *topology* from real data into CARLA, and you'd still be missing real camera/LiDAR appearance to match against). If your project's action space is throttle/steer/brake against a rendered 3D world, comma2k19's raw camera data is actually the closer match among everything surveyed here, precisely because it's real pixels rather than vectorized state — worth keeping this alignment in mind rather than assuming any dataset drops cleanly into any simulator.
