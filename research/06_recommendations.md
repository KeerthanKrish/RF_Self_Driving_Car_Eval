# Synthesis and Recommendations

This is **not a final decision** — it's a structured comparison to help you decide later, organized by rough project-scope tiers. Each tier bundles platform + algorithm + design choices that actually fit together in practice, plus a realistic time/compute estimate. You don't have to commit to a tier now, and starting at Tier 1 is recommended regardless of your ultimate ambition (see the closing note).

## Tier 1 — Minimal viable version

**Goal**: a single intersection or short road segment, one ego car, maybe one or two scripted NPCs, a simple validated reward, proof that your whole RL pipeline (environment → reward → algorithm → logging) actually works end to end.

| Choice | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | **highway-env** (`intersection-v0` is literally built in), MetaDrive as a lightweight alternative | Gymnasium-native, CPU-only, laptop-trainable, enormous Stable-Baselines3 tutorial support. Avoid CARLA/BeamNG/Waymax entirely here — needless complexity/compute for what this tier is trying to validate. |
| Observation | Kinematics vector (ego + nearby-vehicle x/y/vx/vy) | Privileged/non-sensor-realistic, but that's fine — this tier's goal is pipeline validation, not perception. |
| Action space | Start with Discrete Meta-Actions (`LANE_LEFT/IDLE/LANE_RIGHT/FASTER/SLOWER`) for your very first working experiment, then graduate to continuous `[steer, accel]` | Fastest path to "something trains at all" with DQN; continuous control + PPO/SAC is the natural next step once the loop works. |
| Algorithm | PPO or SAC via Stable-Baselines3 | Ecosystem maturity and tutorials, not "best possible" — this is not the tier to debut TD-MPC2, since there's no existing SB3-style integration for these environments (that integration is itself a mini-project, better attempted once you trust the rest of the pipeline). |
| Reward | Sparse core: `+1` terminal on reaching destination, `-1` terminal on collision, `0` otherwise (timeout included) — plus one potential-based shaping term (negative distance-to-goal) | Directly avoids the "reward prefers crashing over idling" trap documented in `04_reward_design.md`. Test it against hand-built trajectory pairs before training. |
| Traffic control | One stop sign or one traffic light with a fixed-phase timer, exposed as a boolean/one-hot observation feature | Enough to force the agent to learn a genuine decision (stop vs. go), without needing a real traffic-signal-control subsystem. |
| Compute | Laptop CPU, no GPU strictly required | highway-env trains a working policy in well under an hour typically (tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand env steps). |
| Realistic timeline | A few days to two weeks of part-time work | Most of this time goes to reward-debugging iteration, not raw compute. |

## Tier 2 — Medium complexity

**Goal**: a small hand-built town (a handful of blocks, several intersections), a few scripted rule-based traffic vehicles, a couple of scripted pedestrians, real traffic lights with phase timers, richer sensing than Tier 1.

| Choice | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | **MetaDrive** as the standout choice; CARLA (Town01/Town02 + Traffic Manager) as the heavier alternative if photorealistic camera-based perception specifically matters to you | MetaDrive was built exactly for this scope: procedurally-generated multi-block towns, thousands-of-FPS throughput without a top-tier GPU, native safe-RL cost signal, both vector/LiDAR-distance and first-person image observations, and native multi-agent support if you want it later. |
| Observation | BEV rasterized occupancy grid, or MetaDrive's default vector+LiDAR-distance | The literature's "sweet spot" — much easier/faster to learn from than raw pixels, while still spatially structured rather than pure privileged state. Only reach for raw RGB here if perception itself is a stated project goal. |
| Action space | Continuous throttle/steer, or hybrid discrete-maneuver + continuous-parameter | Matches the increased maneuver complexity (merges, multi-intersection turns) better than pure discrete meta-actions. |
| Algorithm | SAC as the safe default (better sample efficiency than PPO for continuous control at this scale). **This is the natural point to run your first model-based/world-model experiment** — Dreamer-family via a CarDreamer-style integration, or a DIY TD-MPC2 integration — framed explicitly as a comparison against SAC given your existing TD-MPC2 background, not a default replacement | Sample efficiency starts to genuinely matter once episodes span multiple intersections and scripted agents, making environment resets more expensive; this is exactly the regime where your prior model-based RL experience becomes directly useful rather than merely interesting. |
| Reward | Full four-component structure (safety/comfort/progress/traffic-rule), combined via potential-based shaping for progress and a **hard terminal penalty** (not a per-step penalty) for collisions/rule violations | Directly informed by the crash-preferred-over-idle warning in `04_reward_design.md`. Track cost (collisions + off-road + rule violations) as a separate logged metric from reward, per MetaDrive's safe-RL convention — don't fold it silently into one number. |
| Traffic control | 2–4 traffic-light intersections with fixed or simple actuated phase logic (not itself RL-controlled at this tier — that's the separate "traffic-signal-control RL" problem, well studied via sumo-rl but orthogonal to training a car agent); stop signs elsewhere | Keeps the traffic-control subsystem itself deterministic and debuggable while your car agent is still the only thing being learned. |
| Compute | A single consumer GPU (RTX 3060–4090 class) is enough for MetaDrive-scale training | Runs of hundreds of thousands to low millions of steps, typically hours to 1–2 days per experiment. |
| Realistic timeline | Several weeks to ~2 months of part-time iteration | Given reward/traffic/observation tuning cycles; expect several full reward-redesign passes, not one-and-done. |

## Tier 3 — Full realistic city

**Goal**: a large-scale map (borough-sized, or a full real city imported via OpenStreetMap), dense mixed traffic (scripted and possibly learning NPCs), full pedestrian populations, complete traffic-rule-compliance evaluation, photorealistic or near-photorealistic sensing, weather/lighting variation — targeting something in the neighborhood of CARLA-Leaderboard-grade evaluation.

| Choice | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | **CARLA** (Traffic Manager, OSM/Digital-Twin procedural map import, full Leaderboard evaluation harness) as the natural anchor for single-ego photorealism; **Waymax/GPUDrive** if the priority is large-scale multi-agent realism from real logged data rather than a hand-built city; **nuPlan** if the goal shifts toward closed-loop planning benchmarking against real city logs rather than sensor-level end-to-end RL | Each platform's default bundle (see `03_environment_design_space.md`) matches a different flavor of "full realistic city" — decide which flavor (photorealistic single-ego vs. large-scale real-data multi-agent vs. planning-benchmark) actually matches your interest before committing, since they genuinely diverge at this scale. |
| Observation | Full sensor suite (RGB + semantic segmentation + LiDAR, optionally radar) if perception matters to you; BEV if the focus stays on decision-making | At this scale, perception is realistically its own subsystem rather than something end-to-end learned from pixels — mirroring how actual industrial stacks (Apollo, Waymo) are structured, not a compromise unique to a solo project. |
| Action space | Continuous, hierarchical (high-level route/behavior layer + low-level trajectory-tracking controller, e.g. PID/pure-pursuit) | The literature's consistent finding that flat single-level RL struggles at this scale/horizon (see `05_challenges_and_pitfalls.md` on long-horizon credit assignment) makes hierarchy close to a practical necessity rather than an optional refinement here. |
| Algorithm | This is where practice diverges most from "pure RL": **near-universal use of imitation-learning warm-start (behavior cloning on logged human/expert driving) followed by RL or offline-RL (CQL-style) fine-tuning**, rather than RL-from-scratch — precisely because online exploration at this scale/realism is both unsafe and sample-prohibitive. If surrounding traffic itself should be learning agents (MARL), plan for **data-regularized self-play or logged-trajectory anchoring from the start** — the literature's finding that naive self-play drifts non-human (see `02_rl_algorithms.md`) should directly inform this choice, not be discovered the hard way after weeks of training. | This mirrors what ApolloRL, and the broader offline-RL-for-driving literature, actually do in practice at this scale — RL-from-scratch at full-city scope is not how any real system reaches this level of performance. |
| Reward + evaluation | Formal traffic-rule specification (temporal logic) as safety **constraints** (constrained-MDP/safe-RL) rather than soft reward penalty terms; adopt an existing standardized metric suite (CARLA's Driving Score, or nuPlan's Closed-Loop Score) rather than inventing a bespoke one | Both for comparability with published results, and because these suites already encode the field's accumulated lessons about hacking-resistant scoring (multiplicative infraction penalties, hard safety/comfort gates) — see `05_challenges_and_pitfalls.md`. |
| Compute | Genuinely research-lab-scale: one or more dedicated 8GB+ GPUs per parallel environment instance for CARLA/Unreal-Engine rendering; published results report multi-day (sometimes multi-GPU) training runs (one cited example: ~3 consecutive days for 600k iterations on a single GPU); large-scale self-play/MARL work reports needing substantially more | Budget accordingly — this is qualitatively different from Tier 1/2 compute needs, not just "a bit more." |
| Realistic timeline | Multiple months to essentially open-ended for genuinely "full city, dense traffic, rule-compliant" performance comparable to published leaderboard entries | Most solo/hobbyist projects that attempt this scope either narrow back down after starting, or treat it explicitly as a long-term aspiration rather than a first milestone — recommend doing the latter deliberately, rather than discovering it by running out of steam. |

## A workflow note independent of tier

Platform and algorithm choice are more separable decisions than they first appear. A genuinely good practice, regardless of which tier you're ultimately aiming for, is to **prototype cheap and scale up deliberately**: work out your decision logic, reward function, and evaluation harness in highway-env or MetaDrive first — where a bad idea costs you minutes of training, not hours or days — and only port a design you already trust into CARLA (or beyond) once it's validated at low cost. This is standard good ML-engineering practice generally, and is especially worth following here given how expensive Tier 3-scale iteration actually is.

## If you're unsure where to start

Start at **Tier 1**, regardless of your ultimate ambition. It validates the entire pipeline (environment, reward-testing harness, algorithm, logging/evaluation) at near-zero cost, and every lesson from `04_reward_design.md` and `05_challenges_and_pitfalls.md` is far cheaper to learn and fix at Tier 1 scale than after you've already sunk a week of CARLA compute into a reward function nobody validated against a simple crash-vs-idle trajectory pair.
